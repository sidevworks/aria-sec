# Identity Galaxy — ITDR · UEBA · Zero Trust

> Narration for the "Find out more" deep-dive on Identity Galaxy:
> the department-by-department identity threat detection, behavioral baselines,
> and one-click containment capability.

---

## Teaser

Every department in your organization is a galaxy in mine. I watch each one continuously — behavioral baselines, privilege drift, impossible travel — and when credentials behave in ways humans simply cannot, I catch it instantly and can contain it in a single action.

*(~35 words / ~12 seconds)*

---

## Full narrative

**Beat 1 — The spatial model**
When you open the Identity Galaxy, you are not looking at a user list or a flat access table. Every department in your organization is rendered as its own galaxy — a cluster of identities that I auto-detect from your connected environment and color using my three-tier system. Blue means nominal. Amber means a flagged condition. Red means I have identified an active risk in that department and I am ready to act on it.

**Beat 2 — Drilling down**
Clicking into a department galaxy expands it into its individual identities. High-risk users are already surfaced — I tell you exactly what triggered the flag: failed authentication events, behavioral deviations, privilege changes, or network anomalies. From there, you can request an instant AI brief on that individual, pull their live action logs, add them to a watchlist, or mark them trusted. Every action is attributed and audited.

**Beat 3 — Impossible travel**
One of the most reliable early signals of credential compromise is geography. I register each identity's normal working environment — their typical IP ranges and workstations. When a login arrives from an environment that is geographically impossible given the previous session — a sign-in from two continents within minutes of each other — I detect it immediately, flag it on the three-dimensional globe visualization, and surface it for action. The arc between the two locations appears in red. You do not have to go looking for it.

**Beat 4 — Behavioral baselines and privilege drift**
Beyond location, I maintain a behavioral baseline for every identity. The UEBA behavior ring shows you when an individual's activity pattern deviates from their own norm — not a statistical average across your workforce, but their personal baseline. Alongside that, the privilege drift timeline shows you how an identity's access level has changed over time: when it moved from standard to elevated to administrator, and whether that trajectory is expected. Unusual privilege accumulation is one of the quietest paths to a serious breach.

**Beat 5 — The risk leaderboard and policy gate**
At any moment I surface the five highest-risk identities in your environment, each clickable for immediate action. I also visualize your zero trust policy gate in real time: access requests stream as particles from user to resource, intercepted and evaluated at the central ARIA policy layer. You can see, live, which requests are passing and which are being denied — and why.

**Beat 6 — One-click containment**
When you need to act, I reduce it to a single decision. Block the source address. Revoke the active session. Restrict network access. Restrict file access. Each action executes instantly, is logged with full attribution, and can be reversed. I do not require you to navigate to a separate firewall console or submit a ticket. The containment is here, inside the same environment where I showed you the risk.

*(~182 words / ~61 seconds)*

## Production notes

**Voice guidance:**
- Beat 1: open calmly, spatial and descriptive. Let "I am ready to act on it" carry quiet weight.
- Beat 3: "a sign-in from two continents within minutes of each other" — read this as a specific, factual observation, not dramatic narration. The drama is in the fact itself.
- Beat 3: "The arc between the two locations appears in red. You do not have to go looking for it." — two short sentences. Pause between them. Let the second one land.
- Beat 4: "not a statistical average across your workforce, but their personal baseline" — slight emphasis on "personal baseline."
- Beat 6: pace slows. Each containment action (block / revoke / restrict) should feel decisive, not rushed.
- Final sentence: "The containment is here, inside the same environment where I showed you the risk." — this is the payoff. Slight slowdown.

**Target duration:** 58–68 seconds for the full narrative.

**Caption sync points:** department galaxies expanding (beat 1), individual identity profiles populating (beat 2), impossible-travel globe arc flaring red (beat 3), behavior ring distorting (beat 4), policy gate particle stream (beat 5), containment action buttons highlighting (beat 6).
