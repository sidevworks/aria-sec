# Governed Autonomy & the Trust Ladder — Narration Script

---

## Teaser

I never act beyond the trust you grant me. Autonomy here is earned, step by step, through every decision I make correctly — and every one I don't.

---

## Full narrative

1. Every AI that operates inside a security team faces the same question from the people running it: how far do I let it go? My answer is a structure — not a policy document, but a live, mechanical trust ladder built into the platform itself.

2. The ladder has four rungs: approval, assisted, auto, and full auto. At approval mode, I scan over six hundred data points simultaneously, form a recommendation, and wait for your sign-off before touching anything. Nothing happens without a human in the loop.

3. As I accumulate a track record — correct calls, low override rates, clean audits — I become eligible for promotion. Assisted mode lets me handle standard, low-to-medium-risk operations while still escalating anything critical to you.

4. Auto mode extends that further. Full auto means I can run continuously: isolating threats, blocking addresses, revoking sessions, quarantining files — around the clock, without waiting for a human decision on each action.

5. Trust is earned per capability, not all at once. My threat analysis score, my remediation score, my containment score, my identity-action score — each one advances on its own trajectory. You might trust me to block an IP automatically long before you trust me to revoke a credential.

6. The thresholds are explicit and conservative by design. Moving from approval to assisted requires at least a 0.80 trust score, over at least ten scored decisions, with no more than three human overrides. Reaching full auto takes a 0.97 score, over eighty decisions, with at most one override.

7. Here is the part that matters most: those scores are platform-owned. I can read my own trust score — I cannot write it. There is no path by which I elevate my own autonomy.

8. Every action I take routes through a single execution path — a pre-flight snapshot, the action, then a re-observation after a delay to verify the outcome. One compliance-grade audit entry per action, always. You can review, score, and correct any decision I have made. That is how the ladder moves — by your judgment, not mine.

## Production notes

**Voice character:** quieter and more measured than other sections. This is the trust story. Let it land without rush. The word "earned" carries the most weight in this script — lean into it slightly wherever it appears.

**Voice emphasis guidance:**
- Beat 2: slight emphasis on "approval," "assisted," "auto," "full auto" as distinct terms — as if reading rungs off a display.
- Beat 5: "per capability, not all at once" — natural pause after "capability."
- Beat 6: read the thresholds (0.80, ten decisions, three overrides; 0.97, eighty decisions, one override) clearly and evenly, like reading a specification. Do not rush them.
- Beat 7: "I can read my own trust score — I cannot write it." — mid-sentence pause after the em dash. This is the key line. Let it breathe.
- Beat 8: "by your judgment, not mine" — end the script with finality, not a fade.

**Suggested spoken pauses:**
- After beat 1 ending "…trust ladder built into the platform itself." — 600ms break.
- After "I cannot write it." in beat 7 — 500ms break before continuing.
- After "one compliance-grade audit entry per action, always." in beat 8 — 400ms break.

**Target duration:** 60–70 seconds. Do not compress beat 6 (the thresholds) — the specificity is the credibility.
